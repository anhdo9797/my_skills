## Keyword & Search Intent Research

The goal is to find categories where **search demand is high but quality supply is low**.

### Search Strategy

1. **App Store keyword research**
   - Search core category terms in App Store / Google Play
   - Note autocomplete suggestions — these reveal real user queries
   - Look for long-tail keywords with fewer results (e.g., "meditation for insomnia"
     vs. "meditation app")

2. **Google Trends analysis**
   - Compare interest over time for category terms
   - Check "Related queries" → Rising section for emerging demand
   - Filter by target geography
   - Look for consistent upward trends, not seasonal spikes (unless targeting seasonal)

3. **ASO tool data** (if user has access)
   - AppTweak, ASODesk, MobileAction, Sensor Tower
   - Focus on: Search Volume vs. Difficulty ratio
   - High volume + low difficulty = opportunity signal

### What to look for
- **Rising search terms** with few quality results in app stores
- **Category gaps**: subcategories that don't have a dedicated top app
- **Cross-market gaps**: popular search terms in US/EU without equivalent in target market

## Trend Scanning Sources

Use web search to check these sources for current data:

| Source | What to look for |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Google Trends** | Rising search queries in app-related categories |
| **Product Hunt** | Recently launched apps gaining traction in relevant categories |
| **Reddit** (r/androidapps, r/iphone, r/SideProject) | "What app do you wish existed?" threads |
| **App Store "Today" tab** | Editor picks and featured apps — signal category attention |
| **TechCrunch / The Verge** | Emerging tech categories (AI tools, health tech, etc.) |
| **SensorTower / data.ai blog** | Quarterly app market reports with category growth data |
| **Y Combinator's RFS** | "Requests for Startups" — signals where smart money sees opportunity |
| **TikTok / YouTube search** | "Best apps for..." videos — reflects real consumer interest |

### Global Trend Scanning Pattern

Apps often succeed by being **first to localize a proven concept** from another market:

1. Search "top apps [category] [US/Japan/Korea]"
2. Check if equivalent exists in the target market
3. If not → potential localization opportunity
4. Validate that the concept transfers culturally

## TAM-SAM-SOM Framework

Use this to size the market and make your case more concrete:

```
TAM (Total Addressable Market)
  → Total global revenue/users in the broad category
  Example: "The global meditation app market is $4.2B"

SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)
  → The portion you can actually reach given your platform, geography, language
  Example: "Vietnamese-language meditation apps on iOS/Android: ~$15M"

SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)
  → Realistic share you can capture in 12-18 months
  Example: "With aggressive ASO and content marketing: $200K-500K/year"
```

### How to estimate
- Search "[category] market size [year]" for TAM data (Statista, Grand View Research)
- Apply geographic + platform filters for SAM
- Use competitor download/revenue data to calibrate SOM realistically
- Be conservative — if you can't defend your SOM estimate, cut it in half

## Review Mining for Opportunity Signals

Negative reviews of existing apps are **gold** for finding opportunities:

### Mining Process
1. Find top 5-10 apps in the niche
2. Sort reviews by "Most Recent" + filter 1-3 stars
3. Read 30-50 negative reviews per app
4. Categorize complaints into themes

### Common complaint patterns that signal opportunity

| Complaint Type | Opportunity Signal |
|---------------|-------------------|
| "Too complicated" | Simpler UX for a specific audience |
| "Too many ads" | Ad-free or less aggressive monetization |
| "Missing [specific feature]" | Feature gap to fill |
| "Subscription too expensive" | Better pricing model |
| "Doesn't work offline" | Offline-first approach |
| "Not available in my language" | Localization opportunity |
| "Privacy concerns" | Privacy-focused alternative |
| "Slow / crashes" | Technical quality as differentiator |
| "Not updated anymore" | Abandoned app with active demand |

### Red flags — when NOT to enter
- Very few reviews overall (no demand, not just no supply)
- Complaints are about the fundamental concept, not execution
- Top apps have 4.5+ stars and millions of reviews (hard to beat)

## Problem-First Discovery

Instead of starting with "what app to build", start with "what problems do people have":

### Discovery channels
1. **Reddit search**: "[problem area] frustrating", "wish there was an app"
2. **Quora / StackExchange**: Questions about solving specific problems
3. **Twitter/X search**: Complaints about existing tools
4. **Facebook Groups**: Niche communities discussing pain points
5. **YouTube comments**: Under "best apps for X" videos

### Problem validation criteria
- **Frequency**: How often does this problem occur? (Daily > Weekly > Monthly)
- **Intensity**: How painful is it? (Major frustration > Minor annoyance)
- **Existing solutions**: What do people currently use? How bad are they?
- **Willingness to pay**: Are people spending money on partial solutions?

## Tech-Enabled Opportunities

Watch for categories where new technology creates new possibilities:

| Technology | Opportunity Areas |
|-----------|------------------|
| **On-device AI/ML** | Smart photo editing, real-time translation, personal assistants |
| **AR/VR** | Interior design, education, gaming, fitness form checking |
| **Health sensors** | Sleep tracking, stress monitoring, women's health |
| **Offline-first** | Travel tools, field work apps, education in low-connectivity areas |
| **Voice UI** | Hands-free tools, accessibility, cooking/workout apps |
| **IoT integration** | Smart home management, pet monitoring, garden care |

The sweet spot: **established problem + new tech capability = fresh solution**.

## Niche Evaluation Checklist

Before moving a niche to Phase 2, it should pass these checks:

- [ ] **Demand signal exists** — Real search volume, forum discussions, or competitor revenue
- [ ] **Competitors have weaknesses** — Reviews or feature gaps suggest room for improvement
- [ ] **Team can execute** — Tech stack supports the core features
- [ ] **Monetization path clear** — At least one plausible revenue model
- [ ] **Not a passing fad** — Trend is sustained, not a one-time spike
- [ ] **Market accessible** — Language, cultural, and regulatory barriers are manageable

Score: at least 4/6 checks should pass to advance to Phase 2.
